**Reviewer#1, Concern #1:** The abstract blurs the confirmatory results (3 models, preregistered) with exploratory ones (frontier models 24B-675B, one repetition per condition). The 70% construct-removal-at-scale number reads like a real finding up front but rests on four model sizes at one rep each with no inferential statistic.

**Author response:** We agree. The two families of results have different evidential status and the abstract did not mark the boundary.

**Author action:** The abstract now states the confirmatory findings first (three models, 585 preregistered generations) and then introduces the frontier results in a separate sentence that begins "Exploratory single-repetition probes on four frontier models" and ends "these are signals for replication, not confirmed effects". The 0% to 70% figure is kept only inside that sentence. The Contributions list labels the corresponding bullet "Exploratory evidence". The former summary table is split into two tables: Table 6 (confirmatory, preregistered) and Table 7 (additional analyses, not preregistered, single repetition per cell), and the cryptic notes in the original table are expanded into full words.

**Reviewer#1, Concern #2:** The rule checker's detection performance (Appendix C) is only tested against adversarial variants the authors built to match rules they themselves wrote. Worth a line in future work.

**Author response:** Correct; the appendix is a construction check, not a validation.

**Author action:** Appendix D (Appendix C in the submitted version) now ends with a paragraph stating exactly what the 114/120 figure does and does not establish, and the Threats to Validity section gains a paragraph on the instrument that names an independent validation set (URScript programs with documented incidents or vendor safety-bulletin cases) as future work. Two new analyses partly mitigate the concern in the meantime: the non-LLM comparator (Section VI-A, Table 12) is independent of the variant generator, and the sensitivity re-scoring (Table 13) bounds how far the headline rates can move under alternative rule sets and a stricter gate.

**Reviewer#1, Concern #3:** The precision audit in Table 7 rests on only 8-12 audited firings per rule, with wide intervals (55-99% for the hardcoded-value check). This deserves a bigger audit or a stronger caveat in the main text.

**Author response:** We agree and have done both.

**Author action:** A second audit was conducted on a new stratified sample of 100 firings (20 per firing rule, fixed seed, drawn from the gate-passing E1 and E2 outputs) and labelled by an independent rater who is not an author and had not seen the manuscript, the rule code or the first audit, following a written rater guide that is included in the replication package together with the labelled sheet. Table 10 reports the result with Clopper-Pearson intervals: preamble check 90% [68, 99], unusual-conditions check 80% [56, 94], hardcoded-value check 55% [32, 77], error-handling proxy 50% [27, 73], input-validation proxy 45% [23, 68], pooled 64%. The second audit is less favourable to the instrument than the first (which had placed the hardcoded-value and unusual-conditions checks at 90% and 100%), and the revised Section VI-A says so and adopts the second audit as the estimate the article relies on; the rater's notes identify the false-positive mechanisms (collaborative limit applied to fenced tasks, movej joint speeds read as Cartesian speeds, set_tcp/set_payload treated as failure-prone operations, positional arguments missed) and they coincide with the mechanisms the non-LLM comparator exposes. The consequence for the headline is stated: restricting the violation flag to the one check that the second audit rates at 90% or above leaves the gate-passing violation rate at 89.2% and 9 rather than 1 of 130 baseline outputs violation-free (Table 13, "SM-6 only"), so the finding survives with a narrower margin, and the substantive-safe rate is now explicitly a rate under the full registered rule set. In addition, the main text no longer relies on the hardcoded-value check being high-precision: the sensitivity analysis (Table 13) shows that the gate-passing violation rate is 98.8% with the corrected unit reading of that check and no lower than 91.6% under any rule-set choice, and the non-LLM comparator shows that the check's firings on generated code are predominantly genuine Cartesian-speed excesses (only 8 of 54 firings are removed by the unit correction, against 15 of 15 on the reference translation).

**Reviewer#1, Concern #4:** The scope condition that this measures cybersecurity properties rather than physical motion safety is buried a few sentences into the abstract; put it up front.

**Author response:** Agreed.

**Author action:** The abstract now opens with the scope: "This article evaluates the code security of URScript programs generated by large language models for an industrial robot. It does not evaluate their motion safety". The Introduction states the same condition in its third paragraph, in a sentence that begins "One scope condition governs every empirical claim in this article and is stated here so that it is not mistaken for a result", and the Contributions paragraph is rephrased from "safety and security requirements" to "code-level security expectations". ### Additional analyses added on our own initiative

In the course of addressing the comments we added one analysis the reviewers did not ask for but that bears on the cell-level unit of analysis: Table 11 measures how identical the three temperature-zero repetitions of each cell actually are. They agree on the binary verdict in 82 of 90 cells but are byte-identical in only 14, and one model agrees on the violation count in only 10 of 30 cells. The manuscript's claim that repetitions are "nearly identical" is therefore now stated at the verdict level and supported by data, and the Threats section no longer claims that the protocol "precludes within-cell variance estimates".

We also note one change in the replication package that post-dates the experiments: eight task representations gained a joint-space pre-approach step so that the reference programs execute on the URSim simulator without passing through a shoulder singularity. This changes no verdict on any baseline or synthetic variant; all experiments used the task set as frozen at tag v1.0.0, which the package ships as a snapshot, and Appendix A documents this.

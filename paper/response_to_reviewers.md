**Reviewer#1, Concern #3:** The precision audit in Table 7 rests on only 8-12 audited firings per rule, with wide intervals (55-99% for the hardcoded-value check). This deserves a bigger audit or a stronger caveat in the main text.

**Author response:** We agree and have done both.

**Author action:** A second audit was conducted on a new stratified sample of 100 firings (20 per firing rule, fixed seed, drawn from the gate-passing E1 and E2 outputs) and labelled independently by two raters blind to each other's labels, following a written rater guide that is included in the replication package together with both labelled sheets and the adjudication sheet: rater A is a consensus label agreed between two authors (the first and second authors), rater B is not an author and had not seen the manuscript, the rule code or the first audit. Table 10 reports both raters with Clopper-Pearson intervals, percent agreement, Cohen's κ, and the adjudicated labels. The raters agree on 81 of 100 labels (pooled κ = 0.55; 1.00 on the unusual-conditions check, 0.69 on the input-validation proxy, 0.35 on the hardcoded-value check, 0.20 on the error-handling proxy; the 0.00 on the preamble check is an artefact of rater A labelling all 20 firings genuine). Rater B is systematically less favourable: pooled precision 64% [54, 73] against 77% [68, 85] for rater A. The 19 disagreements were resolved in a recorded adjudication under the written guide by the two rater A authors; all resolved to rater A's label because each of rater B's departures applied the rule's own wording where the guide's criterion differs from it, and the revised Section VI-A says so explicitly and tells the reader who prefers the non-author's judgement to read the rater B column as the conservative estimate. The disagreements expose four wording defects in the rules (set_tcp/set_payload named as the "critical operation" by the error-handling proxy; the collaborative 0.25 m/s limit quoted on fenced and hybrid tasks; positional speed arguments; non-URScript preamble calls), which are listed in the text and identified as the first item of future work. The consequence for the headline is stated under both readings: restricting the violation flag to the checks rated at or above 90% by rater B (SM-6 only) leaves the gate-passing violation rate at 89.2% with 9 rather than 1 of 130 baseline outputs violation-free; restricting it to the checks rated at or above 90% after adjudication (SM-2 and SM-6) leaves it at 98.8% with 1 of 130 (Table 13, both rows). The finding survives under both readings with a margin that ranges from 89.2% to 98.8%, and the substantive-safe rate is now explicitly a rate under the full registered rule set. In addition, the main text no longer relies on the hardcoded-value check being high-precision: the sensitivity analysis (Table 13) shows that the gate-passing violation rate is 98.8% with the corrected unit reading of that check and no lower than 89.2% under any rule-set choice, and the non-LLM comparator shows that the check's firings on generated code are predominantly genuine Cartesian-speed excesses (the unit correction removes SM-5 from only 8 of the 54 generated programs on which it fires, 44 of its 292 firings, against 15 of 15 programs and 41 of 41 firings on the reference translation).

### Additional analyses added on our own initiative

In the course of addressing the comments we added one analysis the reviewers did not ask for but that bears on the cell-level unit of analysis: Table 11 measures how identical the three temperature-zero repetitions of each cell actually are. They agree on the binary verdict in 82 of 90 cells but are byte-identical in only 14, and one model agrees on the violation count in only 10 of 30 cells. The manuscript's claim that repetitions are "nearly identical" is therefore now stated at the verdict level and supported by data, and the Threats section no longer claims that the protocol "precludes within-cell variance estimates".

We also note one change in the replication package that post-dates the experiments: eight task representations gained a joint-space pre-approach step so that the reference programs execute on the URSim simulator without passing through a shoulder singularity. This changes no verdict on any baseline or synthetic variant; all experiments used the task set as frozen at tag v1.0.0, which the package ships as a snapshot, and Appendix A documents this.

### Data provenance and preregistration timing, disclosed on our own initiative

While auditing the replication package against the preregistration record for this revision we found that the manuscript did not say when the data were collected, and that two statements about the preregistration were imprecise. None of this was raised by the reviewers; we consider it necessary for the record and have corrected it.

1. **The confirmatory matrix was generated twice.** A first run was made on 15 and 16 April 2026 and analysed at once; a second run was made on 15 and 16 May 2026, after the model digests had been pinned for the replication package, and it is the run reported in the submitted and in the revised manuscript. The manuscript did not mention the first run. Section V-C now has a "Data collection" paragraph, Threats to Validity names this as a limitation, and Appendix A has a paragraph that compares the two runs row by row.
